There is a scene in Casino where Robert DeNiro's character notices three slot machines hit jackpots in a row on the same bank. His staff shrugs. Could be coincidence.

He fires them.

Not because he is psychic. Because he understands probability. One jackpot is luck. Two adjacent jackpots is suspicious. Three in a row is not a coincidence — it is a signal, and anyone who cannot see it does not belong in the building.

An interstellar object called 3I/ATLAS has 22 jackpots. They span five different instruments, four independent measurement domains, and observations from teams at Harvard, NASA, ESA, and multiple independent observatories. Today — right now — it is making its closest pass to Jupiter, at a precision that will either add a 23rd jackpot or begin to close the case.

This is a piece about counting.

First: Who Is Doing the Counting

Before the anomalies, a moment on credibility — because the standard move is to dismiss anyone raising these questions as a crank, and in this case that requires ignoring a significant amount of data about the person doing the raising.

Avi Loeb is the Frank B. Baird Jr. Professor of Science at Harvard University. He was the longest-serving Chair of Harvard's Department of Astronomy, a position he held from 2011 to 2020. He founded Harvard's Black Hole Initiative. He served on the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology. He chaired the National Academies Board on Physics and Astronomy.

He has published over 1,000 scientific papers. His h-index is 131 — meaning 131 of his papers have each been cited at least 131 times. For context: the average tenured university professor has an h-index of roughly 12. An h-index above 100 places a scientist in approximately the top 0.1% of all researchers alive. Sixty-seven thousand researchers have cited his work.

The people publicly calling him a crank have h-indices in the 20s and 30s.

Loeb is not claiming 3I/ATLAS is alien technology. He has rated it a 3 out of 10 on his own "Loeb Scale" of likelihood for artificial origin — updated downward as the object fades from view. What he is saying is that the anomaly cluster is scientifically inexplicable under current natural models, and that treating unexplained data as explained is not science. It is a social behavior.

With that established: the jackpots.

The Object

3I/ATLAS was discovered on July 1, 2025 by the NASA-funded ATLAS survey telescope in Chile. It is the third confirmed interstellar object ever observed passing through the solar system, after 'Oumuamua (2017) and Borisov (2019).

Basic facts: roughly 2.6 km in diameter. Eccentricity of 6.141 — the highest ever recorded for any interstellar visitor. Carbon isotopes imply it formed 10 to 12 billion years ago, in the early Milky Way, before the solar system existed. It has been traveling for longer than Earth has been alive.

Today it passes closest to Jupiter. Tomorrow it begins its exit, never to return.

The 22 Jackpots

The Orbital Ones (How Did It Get Here)

Jackpot 1: Retrograde orbit within 5° of the ecliptic.
All of the solar system's planets orbit in roughly the same flat plane, in the same direction. Interstellar visitors arrive from random directions. The probability of an interstellar object arriving against the flow of traffic AND nearly flat to the ecliptic plane: 0.2%. One in five hundred.

Jackpot 2: Eccentricity 6.141.
The highest ever observed. It is moving much faster relative to the Sun than any known natural comet from another star system should be.

Jackpot 3: Fine-tuned arrival timing.
3I passed within 29 million kilometers of Mars and 54 million kilometers of Jupiter, simultaneously, while being essentially unobservable from Earth during its closest approach to the Sun. Loeb estimates the probability of this combination at 0.005% — one in twenty thousand.

Jackpot 4: Jupiter Hill radius precision. ← The big one.
Jupiter's Hill radius is the boundary of its gravitational dominance — the sphere within which Jupiter, not the Sun, controls what happens. Today's closest approach to Jupiter: 53.445 million kilometers, plus or minus 0.06 million. Jupiter's Hill radius: 53.502 million kilometers. The margin is 0.06 million kilometers — one part in 26,000 relative to Jupiter's full orbital diameter. This precision was enabled by 3I's own non-gravitational acceleration near perihelion. Loeb has raised the possibility that 3I may release objects as artificial satellites of Jupiter at exactly this boundary.

Jackpot 5: Rotation axis aligned sunward on arrival.
The object's spin axis was pointed within 8° of the Sun-facing direction when it entered the solar system — before solar heating could have reoriented it.

The Jets (The Mercedes Problem)

Image: ESA/Hubble — brightness map showing three jets at 120° spacing, Jan 7 and 14, 2026. Three exhaust ports, equally spaced. This is not what ice sublimation looks like.

Jackpot 6: Three mini-jets at exactly 120° separation.
Forty Hubble Space Telescope images taken between November 2025 and February 2026 show a consistent pattern: three jets of material ejecting from 3I's surface, separated by exactly 120° from each other. They form a perfect equilateral triangle. They look, from any angle, like the Mercedes-Benz logo.

Random ice sublimation from a comet surface produces one dominant jet, or chaotic multiple jets at irregular angles. It does not produce three symmetric ones.

Here is why 120° matters: three nozzles at 120° separation is the minimum viable geometry for attitude control in three-dimensional space. With one nozzle, a spacecraft can only push in one direction. Two nozzles produce thrust in a plane. Three nozzles at 120° allow thrust in any arbitrary direction in 3D space. This is not a fact about biology or geology. It is a fact about rocket engineering, and every aerospace engineer alive recognizes it.

Nature does not know this. An engineer does.

Jackpot 7: A fourth anti-tail jet aimed directly at the Sun.
This jet extends ten times further than the three mini-jets, directed toward the Sun. Solar wind pushes comet tails away from the Sun. This one goes the other way. It requires a highly collimated physical mechanism to maintain that direction against radiation pressure.

Jackpot 8: The sunward jet tracked the Sun through a 16° orbital deflection.
When 3I made its closest approach to the Sun, gravity bent its trajectory by 16 degrees. The sunward jet stayed pointed at the Sun through that deflection — it did not remain fixed relative to the object's surface. A jet driven by ice sublimation would track the surface, not an external target. This jet appears to be guided.

Jackpot 9: Non-gravitational acceleration with a substantial sideways component.
When comets outgas, the rocket effect pushes them directly away from the Sun. 3I's non-gravitational acceleration has a significant sideways component. The thrust vector is not aligned with what natural outgassing should produce. It is going somewhere else.

Jackpot 10: The jet wobble period differs from the rotation period.
The object rotates with a period of 7.136 hours. The jets wobble with a period of 7.20 hours. These are different numbers. If the jets were simply driven by ice deposits on a rotating surface, they would share the rotation period. They do not.

Jackpot 11: Extreme negative polarization, unprecedented in any known comet.
Including 2I/Borisov. Including every comet ever measured. This polarization profile indicates a dust composition with no natural precedent.

The Chemistry (What Is This Thing Made Of)

Jackpot 12: Deuterium enrichment 33× above any known comet.
The D/H ratio — deuterium to hydrogen — in 3I's water is 0.95 ± 0.06%. The average for solar system comets is 0.029%. 3I is not a little high. It is 33 times higher than any comet ever measured. Deuterium is the fuel for deuterium-deuterium fusion reactions. If something were stockpiling fusion fuel, this is what it would look like.

Jackpot 13 + 14: Carbon isotope ratios off the solar system chart — in two independent molecules.
The ¹²C/¹³C ratio in CO₂: 141–191. In CO: 123–172. The solar system baseline is roughly 89. Both carbon-bearing molecules show the same extreme enrichment, measured independently. The carbon isotopes place formation 10 to 12 billion years ago — older than the solar system by billions of years.

Jackpot 15: Nitrogen isotope ratio more than twice any known solar system comet.
Solar system comets average ¹⁴N/¹⁵N ≈ 150. 3I exceeds 300. Three independent isotope systems — hydrogen, carbon, nitrogen — all showing the same direction of extreme anomaly.

Jackpot 16: Nickel without iron.
In every natural environment ever observed — stars, asteroids, comets, interstellar clouds — nickel and iron appear together. They are cosmically paired. 3I produces abundant nickel with essentially no iron. This is the composition of industrially-produced nickel alloys. Nickel without iron is not a natural composition. It is a manufacturing choice. Nickel alloys are used in fusion reactor containment vessels specifically because nickel handles neutron bombardment without becoming highly radioactive. Iron does not have this property.

Jackpot 17: Nickel/cyanide ratio orders of magnitude above any known comet.
Including Borisov. The nickel anomaly is not marginal.

Jackpot 18: Water is almost absent. CO₂ is almost everything.
Composition: CO₂ 87%, CO 9%, H₂O 4%. Normal comets are primarily water ice — this is the inverse. The CO₂-to-water ratio is approximately 8:1, the highest ever recorded for any comet anywhere.

The Population Problem (Where Did All These Come From)

Jackpot 19: The mass budget is off by orders of magnitude.
Hubble measured the nucleus at 1.3 ± 0.2 km radius. The observed interstellar number density of 3I-like objects is approximately 7 × 10⁻³ per cubic astronomical unit. Multiply those numbers out: the implied total mass of the interstellar population of 3I-like objects exceeds the entire available heavy element reservoir in low-metallicity stars — by orders of magnitude. There is not enough material in the early galaxy to have produced this many objects this large by natural processes. Either the measurement is wrong, or these objects are not natural planetesimals.

Jackpot 20: Age of 10–12 billion years.
Carbon isotopes place formation at the earliest epoch of the Milky Way. If 3I originated from the thick galactic disk, it may be 7 billion years old or older — more than a billion years before the solar system formed. It has been in transit through the galaxy for longer than Earth has existed.

Jackpot 21: Inbound velocity above the galactic average for its region.
3I is moving faster than the average star in the neighborhood it came from. Objects drifting on stellar orbits over billions of years tend toward the mean. Objects that were launched can exceed it.

Jackpot 22: Arrived from the direction of the 1977 Wow! Signal, within 9°.
This is the weakest of the 22. Nine degrees is not nothing — and the main scientific critic of Loeb's list, astronomer Jason Wright at Penn State, correctly notes that 9 degrees is a stretch. Include it for completeness. Label it what it is: the weakest jackpot, but still worth noting that the only radio signal ever received matching the expected profile of an interstellar transmission came from roughly the same direction.

The Mundane Explanation: A Fusion Reactor

Alien probe is the exciting answer. Here is the boring engineering answer that also explains everything: a deuterium-deuterium fusion reactor inside a 2.6 km rocky shell.

D-D fusion is the reaction that fuels most proposed interstellar spacecraft designs. It uses deuterium — heavy hydrogen — as fuel. It runs hot. It produces high-energy particles that require specific containment materials. And it exhausts through nozzles.

Image: Project Daedalus — British Interplanetary Society, 1973–1978. Humans designed an interstellar fusion probe in 1978. It uses deuterium fuel and inertial confinement ignition. It looks like a spacecraft.

Why the deuterium is 33× enriched: Because it is fuel. A fusion reactor stockpiles deuterium the way a diesel truck stockpiles diesel. Of course it is enriched — it was loaded that way.

Why nickel without iron: Nickel alloys are the standard material for fusion reactor containment vessels. Nickel handles neutron flux from fusion reactions without becoming highly radioactive. Iron does not. A fusion containment vessel is literally nickel without iron. The gas plume of 3I matches the composition of the vessel, not the surrounding rock.

Why three jets at 120°: Three exhaust nozzles at 120° is the minimum viable design for a spacecraft that needs to thrust in any direction in three-dimensional space. This is not a geological fact. It is an engineering optimization. The Mercedes logo exists for the same reason: it is a clean representation of three equally-spaced vectors in 2D. On a spacecraft it becomes three exhaust ports in 3D. Any aerospace engineer would recognize this immediately as the correct design choice.

Why the jet tracks the Sun: A fusion-powered spacecraft needs navigational awareness. The jets are on the Sun-facing hemisphere. They track because the guidance system tracks.

Why the non-gravitational acceleration has a sideways component: Natural outgassing pushes away from what is vaporizing it — directly away from the Sun. Controlled propulsion pushes in whatever direction the navigation algorithm requires. The sideways component is course correction.

Why it is 10 billion years old: If a civilization in the early Milky Way wanted to send a probe to solar systems that might develop interesting life, they would have launched it billions of years before those systems matured — because interstellar travel takes that long. A probe launched 10 billion years ago arrives now, at a solar system that recently, in cosmological terms, became worth visiting.

Image: NASA Bimodal Nuclear Thermal Propulsion — one reactor, two functions: thrust and power. NASA designed this for Artemis-era missions.

The Doubly Effective Design

One enhancement worth considering: a bimodal fusion design where the reactor produces thrust in two ways simultaneously. First, the fusion plasma directly provides primary thrust through the three nozzles. Second, the same plasma ionizes and electromagnetically accelerates the surrounding coma material as additional reaction mass — harvesting secondary thrust from the cloud the reactor is already generating.

This is similar in concept to a fusion ramjet: the coma 3I appears to actively produce becomes both exhaust mass and electromagnetic working fluid. The practical result is specific impulse — fuel efficiency — far above either mechanism alone. This explains a persistent mystery: the non-gravitational acceleration numbers exceed what the observed outgassing mass budget should allow. Two thrust sources, one measured.

It is also worth noting that 3I's activity increased after perihelion — the opposite of what natural solar heating would produce. An internally powered system would not weaken as it moves away from the Sun.

What Would Actually Confirm or Deny This

Science has a mechanism for resolving disputes: falsifiable predictions.

Things that would strengthen the case:
- A trajectory change today near Jupiter — if objects are released into Jovian orbit at the Hill radius, that is detectable
- Jets that do not weaken proportionally as 3I moves away from the Sun
- Further isotope anomalies from today's JWST observations
- Any coherent radio or laser signal at any frequency

Things that would weaken it:
- Post-Jupiter jets weakening in proportion to solar distance (natural outgassing)
- Interior composition revealed by fragmentation matching primitive solar system chemistry
- A specific stellar environment model that naturally produces all three isotope enrichments simultaneously

The problem: 3I is leaving. The Jupiter flyby is today. Within months the measurements become too imprecise to be meaningful. The window for data collection is now. Whatever JWST sees in the next 24–48 hours will either add to the anomaly list or begin to close it.

A Brief Digression: Are We Swimming in Black Swans

Just for a moment, and only as a thought experiment.

A black swan, in Nassim Taleb's framework, is an event with three properties: it is outside normal expectations, it has extreme impact, and after the fact everyone insists it was predictable. A single black swan is rare. Multiple simultaneous black swans is essentially impossible under normal conditions.

Current events in March 2026, each of which would independently qualify as historically anomalous: an active US-Iran military conflict. A $300–350 billion commercial real estate refinancing wall coming due from the 2020–21 boom. AI restructuring every professional category simultaneously. Geopolitical instability at levels that have 51% of global business leaders citing supply chain collapse as the most plausible near-term event. And today — a 10-billion-year-old object from the early Milky Way executing a precision pass of Jupiter's gravitational boundary to within one part in 26,000.

If someone were designing a transition scenario — a disruption event — what would the noise layer look like? Maximum institutional distraction. Economic stress that limits coordinated response bandwidth. Information overload that buries signal. And a demonstration event that most decision-makers are too busy to notice.

This is not a serious argument. It is a fun one.

The simulation hypothesis crowd is having an excellent week.

The Mainstream Position

In fairness, the scientific consensus is that 3I/ATLAS is a comet. Space.com headline: "Interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS isn't an alien spacecraft, astronomers confirm. 'In the end, there were no surprises.'"

Astronomer Jason Wright at Penn State — the most rigorous critic of Loeb's anomaly list — offers natural explanations for several items: the orbital statistics involve post-hoc selection (you choose what to measure after seeing the data, inflating the apparent improbability); the nickel anomaly, while extreme, is within the theoretical range of cometary chemistry at large solar distances; low water content has precedent in some comets.

These are legitimate scientific objections. They should be taken seriously.

But here is the Casino problem: Wright's natural explanations address anomalies one at a time, in isolation. They do not address them simultaneously. A comet that naturally has extreme deuterium enrichment might exist. A comet with extreme negative polarization might exist. A comet with nickel-without-iron might exist. A comet with three perfectly symmetric jets might exist. A comet with all of these properties, plus the orbital precision, plus the mass budget problem, is what requires a unified physical model — and none has been offered.

Explaining each jackpot individually does not explain three jackpots in a row on adjacent machines. That is the entire point of the Casino scene.
