Week 1 of the paper portfolio opened rough. Two losses on Monday night — Wisconsin couldn't cover at Ohio State, UCLA couldn't cover at Michigan State. The Tier B strategy was 0-for-2, and the bankroll was sitting at $985.

Then Tuesday happened.

The Feb 18 Sweep

Three games. Three covers. The model called all three correctly — not just the winner, but the magnitude.

The chart above tells the story. In every Feb 18 game, the market was pricing the underdog too generously for the favorite. Our model flagged each gap. We took the dogs.

Creighton +17.5 at #5 UConn

UConn was a 17.5-point favorite at home. A defending national champion playing on its own court against an unranked Creighton team. That's the kind of number built by public money and narrative, not math. Our model put the fair line at UConn -9.4 — an 8-point gap. The result: Creighton didn't just cover, they won outright. Final: Creighton 91, UConn 84. Cover margin: +24.5 points.

BYU +12.5 at #4 Arizona

Model had Arizona -7.3. Market had Arizona -12.5. BYU's AJ Dybantsa put up 35 points in a losing effort — Arizona won 75-68. BYU lost by 7. Cover margin: +5.5 points.

Arkansas +4.5 at #25 Alabama

The smallest gap — model -2.9 vs market -4.5, a 1.6-point edge — but it required the most drama to validate. Darius Acuff Jr. scored 49 points (second-most in Arkansas program history) and played all 50 minutes. He sent the game to overtime with a buzzer-tying three. Then to double overtime. Alabama finally won 117-115. Arkansas lost by 2. Cover margin: +2.5 points.

Three games. The market was wrong on the margin in every single one.

Bankroll Recovery

The bankroll curve shows exactly what Tier B is designed to do: absorb early variance, recover systematically. After the Feb 17 losses we were down $15. After the Feb 18 sweep we're at 4-2-0, $1,020.

The two losses came from picks where the model and market broadly agreed on the winner — those are noisier. The three wins came from picks where the market dramatically overpriced the home favorite. That gap is the signal the model is built to find.

What Made Feb 18 Different

The three games shared a pattern: marquee home favorites (defending-champ UConn, top-5 Arizona, ranked Alabama) absorbing heavy public money. When the public loads up on a side, the line moves. When the line moves past what the math supports, that's where the model finds edge.

This is the Tier B thesis — not "bet the underdog," not "fade the favorite." Specifically: when the market prices a favorite significantly more confidently than our Elo model does, take the points.

Three games in, it's 3-for-3 on that hypothesis.
